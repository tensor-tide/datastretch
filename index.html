For Android

Make your mobile data last the whole month.

When your use runs ahead of what your plan allows today, Data Pace lowers the download bandwidth on the phone. Most adaptive streaming players respond to the lower throughput by picking a lighter rendition, which needs less data per minute, and data-heavy background processes can wait for Wi-Fi. Auto mode sets the rate from your remaining budget and days; your per-app rules handle the rest.

How it works

  1. Set your budget

    Enter your allowance and its cycle: a monthly plan with its start day, or a prepaid package of 1 to 365 days.

  2. See where it goes

    Data Pace reads Android's own network statistics per app and per day, and fills in up to three earlier cycles at setup.

  3. Stay on pace

    Auto mode spreads the remaining budget over the remaining days. When today's use runs ahead, a local VPN on the phone lowers the download rate in steps, and your rules decide which apps keep their background data.

What you get

Lighter video

At a lower download rate, most adaptive streaming players pick a lighter rendition, which needs less data per minute. Once you are back on pace, the rate rises again.

Background on Wi-Fi

Set Wi-Fi only or Only while on screen per app, or block background downloads on mobile for all apps. Messaging apps are Essential and keep their background data.

Auto mode and rules

Auto paces in four steps from Fast to Essential, or you set a fixed rate from 8 kbit/s to 1 Gbit/s. Per-app speed limits, priorities and budgets apply to TCP and UDP, QUIC included.

Per-app counts

Data Pace counts mobile data per app and per day from Android's own statistics, keeps 13 months of history on the phone and exports it as CSV.

Advanced mode

It shows throughput, time at each speed, connection setup latency and jitter, DNS lookup times and signs of packet loss, measured from traffic that passes anyway, with no probes.

Widgets and tile

Five home-screen widgets and a Quick Settings tile show where you stand; one widget also sets the speed. A switch for each kind of notification decides what reaches you.

Questions

Why does Data Pace need a VPN?

Android lets an app shape other apps' traffic only through its VpnService interface. Data Pace's VPN is a local tunnel, a userspace network stack with a local relay, and has no remote endpoint: traffic leaves the phone through your carrier or Wi-Fi, never through a server of ours.

Can I still use another VPN?

Android runs one VPN at a time. While another VPN app is active, Data Pace keeps counting but cannot pace, and it never takes the connection back by itself.

Does it read or change my traffic?

It never reads or modifies payloads, and it does not compress, transcode or cache. It sees what pacing needs: which app opened a connection, the remote address, the byte counts, and how long connection setup and name lookups take. Server names from DNS answers and TLS handshakes are read only if you switch on Destinations per app, and stay on the phone for the current cycle.

What happens after a reboot?

If pacing was on, it resumes by itself after a reboot or an update of Data Pace.

Does it count Wi-Fi?

Data Pace counts and paces mobile data, since that is what your plan limits; on Wi-Fi it neither counts nor slows anything. If your Wi-Fi is metered too, switch on Treat Wi-Fi as mobile data in Settings; the tunnel then reports Wi-Fi as metered as well.
