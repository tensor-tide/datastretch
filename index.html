Data Stretch helps you stay within a monthly or prepaid data allowance. Auto mode adjusts bandwidth to your remaining budget, while app rules let you choose what can use mobile data in the background.

Use less data when streaming video

Video quality has a direct effect on data use. Adaptive video players can respond to lower bandwidth by choosing a lower bitrate, using less data per minute.

Data Stretch controls the bandwidth available to your apps. Auto mode adjusts it as you use your allowance, or you can set a limit yourself. The video app chooses the playback quality; results depend on the player and its settings.

Keep background activity on Wi-Fi

Backups, updates and syncing can use mobile data even when you are not using an app. Set rules to restrict background access on mobile data and leave those activities for Wi-Fi.

Choose rules for individual apps or enable the general background restriction. Essential apps remain exempt from that general restriction. You can review and change their priority at any time.

How it works

  1. Set your allowance

    Enter your data budget and renewal date, or the duration of your prepaid package.

  2. Choose your controls

    Use Auto mode, set your own bandwidth limits, and choose which apps can use mobile data in the background.

  3. Follow your usage

    See which apps use your data and how much remains for the rest of your plan.

Auto mode divides your remaining data over the days left. It adjusts bandwidth in stages as your use approaches the day's allowance, taking the time of day into account. Using less today leaves more for the days ahead.

Set bandwidth limits, priorities, daily budgets and budgets for the current billing cycle or package.

Flexible plans

Use a monthly allowance or a prepaid package of 1 to 365 days. Add top-ups and adjust the count to match your carrier.

View data use by app, day, week or month, with 13 months of history and CSV export.

Quick access

Check your allowance with home-screen widgets and control pacing from a widget or the Quick Settings tile.

Bandwidth rules cover TCP and UDP traffic, including QUIC. You can choose Auto mode, a preset speed or a custom limit from 8 kbit/s to 1 Gbit/s.

Your data stays under your control

Data Stretch works entirely on this phone. No login, no server, no cloud.

Data Stretch runs locally on your phone. Its VPN does not route your traffic through a Data Stretch server.

  • No account or login.
  • No ads, analytics or crash-reporting SDK.
  • Usage history is stored on your phone and excluded from cloud backup and device transfer.
  • You choose whether to export or share your records.
  • Name lookups go to your network's own resolvers, as they would without Data Stretch. Only on a network that names none does Data Stretch use Google Public DNS.

The optional Destinations per app feature reads server names from DNS answers and TLS handshakes to show which hosts an app contacts. It is off by default, and those records stay on your phone for the current cycle.

Questions

Will every video use less data?

The effect depends on the video app and its quality settings. Adaptive playback can select a lower bitrate when less bandwidth is available. A player set to a fixed quality may buffer instead. Data Stretch does not compress or re-encode video, and its bandwidth limits also affect other traffic covered by the same rules.

Why does it need a VPN?

Data Stretch uses Android's VpnService to apply traffic rules locally. The tunnel stays on your device; your apps still connect to their usual services. It does not provide a remote VPN server or change your location.

Can I use it with another VPN?

While another VPN is active, Data Stretch can keep counting usage but cannot apply its pacing rules.

Does it change settings inside other apps?

No. Data Stretch controls network access and bandwidth. It does not change a video app's quality setting or a backup app's schedule. Each app decides when to resume its work once access is available.

What happens to an app with a Wi-Fi only rule?

Its background mobile access is restricted. By default, opening the app allows limited mobile access at up to 128 kbit/s, subject to any stricter speed limit. To block that access too, enable “Cut off apps on screen too.”

Does it affect Wi-Fi or uploads?

By default, Wi-Fi traffic is neither counted nor paced. You can include it with “Treat Wi-Fi as mobile data.” Uploads count toward your allowance but are not speed-limited; rules that block an app's access can also prevent uploads.

What else should I know?

Hotspot traffic is counted but not paced. Apps in a work profile are outside Data Stretch's counting and pacing. Speeds are approximate, and Android's usage figures can differ from your carrier's.
