Privacy policy

English · Deutsch · last updated 2 November 2026

Data Pace works entirely on this phone. No login, no server, no cloud. The app has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no third-party software that reports anywhere. It sends nothing about you to us. Your apps' name lookups keep going to your network's own resolver; only on a network that names no resolver at all does Data Pace fall back to a public one, described below.

What the app stores, on your phone only

  • Mobile data counts per app. With the usage access permission, Data Pace reads Android's own network statistics to count how much mobile data each app used, today and over the past cycles. It keeps this history for 13 months in its own database on your phone, then deletes older records.
  • Your settings. The data budget, the cycle, speed limits and rules for apps, notification choices and the appearance.
  • Destinations per app, only if you switch it on. An optional setting, Settings › Your data, about › Destinations per app, records which internet hosts each app talks to and how much data went to each, for the current cycle only. To learn the hosts, the app reads the names in your apps' name lookups and the server name at the start of each encrypted connection, and nothing else of the traffic. It is off by default, the list stays on your phone, and switching it off deletes it.
  • A support log. A short technical log of what the app did, kept for 90 days. When you share it, Data Pace takes out app names, amounts and the budget, and adds the phone model and the Android version.

How pacing works

To slow or hold back downloads, Data Pace runs a local VPN on your phone. Android asks for your consent before it starts. The VPN exists only inside the phone: your traffic passes through the app to the internet through your carrier or Wi-Fi, and never through any server of ours. While pacing is on, your apps keep looking up internet names through your network's own resolver, your carrier's or your Wi-Fi's, as they would without Data Pace. Data Pace does not change your traffic and stores none of its content. It reads only what it needs to pace: which app opened a connection, the address, the amounts, and how long name lookups take. With Destinations per app on, it also reads the host names, as described above.

Google Public DNS, only as a fallback

Only when the network your phone uses names no resolver at all, and only while pacing is on, do your apps' name lookups go to Google Public DNS instead, a service of Google LLC, 1600 Amphitheatre Parkway, Mountain View, CA 94043, USA. Google receives the public IP address your phone uses on the internet and the names your apps look up. Google processes this as its own controller under the Google Public DNS privacy notice, which states that logs with the full IP address are deleted within 24 to 48 hours.

Data Pace itself sends nothing to Google and never sees what Google stores.

What leaves your phone, and only when you choose

  • A shared summary. The app can draw a picture of your month or week in data, with your top apps by name and, if you choose, the amounts. It leaves the phone only when you tap Share and pick an app in Android's share sheet.
  • The support log. You see it first, and it leaves the phone only through the share sheet when you choose to send it.
  • An export. A CSV file of your counts can be saved in your phone's Downloads folder. Data Pace does not upload it.

Permissions

  • Usage access to count mobile data per app and to know which app is on screen.
  • VPN (asked by Android) to pace traffic on the phone.
  • Notifications to tell you about speed changes, the new month and similar events. Each of these can be switched off in the app; the ongoing notification while pacing runs, which Android requires, and the notice that pacing has stopped cannot.
  • Run at startup so pacing resumes after a reboot or an update of the app if it was on.

The app needs internet access only so that your apps' traffic can pass through the local VPN. It makes no connection of its own.

Deleting your data

Settings › Your data, about › Reset Data Pace deletes everything the app keeps: the counts, the destinations, the rules, the budget, the settings and the support log. Uninstalling the app deletes everything it stored, and Android keeps no copy, because the app excludes its data from cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer. A file you exported or an image you shared stays where you put it.

Children

Data Pace is not directed at children. The app collects no personal information for us from anyone.

Wie das Pacing funktioniert

Um Downloads zu drosseln oder zurückzuhalten, betreibt Data Pace ein lokales VPN auf deinem Telefon. Android fragt dich um Zustimmung, bevor es startet. Das VPN existiert nur im Telefon: dein Datenverkehr läuft durch die App über deinen Anbieter oder dein WLAN ins Internet und nie über einen Server von uns. Solange das Pacing läuft, fragen deine Apps Internetnamen weiter über den Namensdienst deines Netzes ab, den deines Anbieters oder deines WLANs, wie ohne Data Pace. Data Pace verändert deinen Datenverkehr nicht und speichert nichts von seinem Inhalt. Sie liest nur, was sie zum Drosseln braucht: welche App eine Verbindung geöffnet hat, die Adresse, die Mengen und wie lange Namensabfragen dauern. Mit eingeschalteten Zielen pro App liest sie zusätzlich die Hostnamen, wie oben beschrieben.
